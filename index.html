Description

Two parametric systems share one Revit model. The tower is Torre Agbar (Ateliers Jean Nouvel, Barcelona, 2005): three parameters shape it and 1,260 façade panels respond.

The people are families too, with a body, a brain and a memory, and a physics engine walks them through the tower's office floor.

02 / 15 · Human

Parametric body

VA Body.rfa

The type is a body group taken from measured people. The instance is one person, with a name, a pose and a mobility. Formulas turn these into what the simulation needs.

Type Adult Male P50 · 5.75 ft · 1.6 ft shoulders · 199.8 lb

Eye height = if(seated, seat + 0.45 · stature, 0.936 · stature)

Clear width = shoulders + 0.25 ft sway; cane +0.5 ft; walker 2.5 ft; wheelchair 32 in

Speed 4.40 ft/s walking; 1.87 ft/s with a walker

03 / 15 · Human

Parametric brain

VA Brain.rfa

A brain type is a profile. A Visitor plans only through rooms it has been in and searches door by door; Staff know the plan.

Memory belongs to the person: a folder that follows the name into every building, so the same person learns a floor.

Visitor curiosity 0.6 · patience 600 s · pre-movement 30 s

Sight a door is known once a line from the eyes reaches it; furniture blocks only if taller than the eyes

04 / 15 · Human

Simulation

Pawn engine

Each person is a disc as wide as its clear width, with its mass and speed, moved by the social force model every 0.02 s. Routes are A* on a 0.5 ft grid sized to that body, so a door narrower than the body stays closed.

Results return to Revit as parameters, a schedule and a map.

m · dv/dt = m (v₀e − v) / τ + Σ fij + Σ fiW

Constants τ = 0.5 s · A = 2000 N · B = 0.08 m

07 / 15 · Tower

Parametric mass

Project1_mass.rfa

Three instance parameters shape the tower: a cylinder of radius R up to z0, then an elliptical dome closing at H. Floors, panels and louvres follow.

zi = z0 + ti (H − z0)

ri = R √(1 − ti²), t = 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8, 0.95, 0.99

Values R 61′-3½″ · H 465′-10½″ · z0 250′-11¾″

08 / 15 · Tower

Responsive envelope

Project1_envelope.rfa

A four-point curtain panel sits on each of the 1,260 cells (36 columns × 35 rows). Each reads its own width W: the louvres tilt with it, and panels narrower than W_dome turn to glass, which makes the dome.

Tilt = TiltMin + (TiltMax − TiltMin) · (W − Wmin) / (Wmax − Wmin)

Glazed = W < W_dome

Rows = rounddown(Height / Pitch)

Values TiltMin 20° · TiltMax 76° · W_dome 9′-2¼″

Revit critique

  • Revit knows doors and rooms, not people. A body, its sight and its memory had to become parameters plus an outside engine.
  • Parameters pass well through nested families, but slabs wait for Update to Face after the mass changes.
  • Curtain-wall doors have no location point, and the API's FBX export drops materials. Both needed workarounds.
